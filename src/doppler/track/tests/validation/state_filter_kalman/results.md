# KalmanStateFilter — validation report

## 1. The object — design and expectations

The third-order loop of [`StateFilter`](../state_filter/results.md) with its gains computed each update from a covariance recursion instead of held: a Kalman filter on a model whose rate random-walks (`sigma_a`), measured through a detector with noise `sigma_e`. The design — the model, the claim that the fixed-gain shape is the continuous-limit optimum and the discrete difference, the stability rectangle and the bound on a high-gain start — is [`docs/design/loop-filter.md`](../../../../../../../docs/design/loop-filter.md) Part III; the contract is `native/inc/doppler/state_filter_kalman/state_filter_kalman_core.h`. It is its own component, calling `StateFilter` for the loop's step, the exact stability and the exact bandwidth. What a caller reads from this report: the gains the steady state gives, the envelope they are stable over, how much of a high-gain start is allowed and what it buys, and what a wrong noise figure costs.

### 2.5 The high-gain start and its cap

An initial frequency uncertainty `sigma_f0` larger than the steady state's own deviation (`sigma_f`) starts the covariance larger and the gains rise for a while. The constructor accepts a start only if the gains along the covariance's whole trajectory stay inside the stability rectangle (`K` 0.5 to 2, delays 0 to 2); the ceiling `START_MAX` is 5. The cap is a function of `bn*t`, measured by the harness's `--check` run: the rule's own cap (a dense walk, searched to 200) and what the constructor accepts (its sampled walk). The two agree to the bisection, and the ceiling binds where the rule's cap is above 5:

|---|---|---|---|
| 4.0e-02 | 5.90 | 5.00 | ceiling |
| 6.0e-02 | 3.77 | 3.77 | rule |
| 1.2e-01 | 1.19 | 1.19 | rule |


The record (the full sweep, `data/caps.csv`, refreshed on purpose with `--csv`, not part of the gate) adds the slow loops: 1e-04: 6.06, 3e-04: 6.06 (the rule's cap), all above the ceiling.

The walk covers 8/(bn*t) updates. That horizon is measured: after a start of 1 to 5 times, at `bn*t` 1e-3 to the bound, the gains are within 1% of their steady values after at most 4.5/(bn*t) updates (`# settle` block of the harness; the full sweep is `data/settle.csv`).

The constants of the walk are measured. The walk tests each next update 1% of the elapsed updates after the last, and at least one update after it, so every one of the first 100 updates is tested (a 1% step is under one update there) and no separate head is needed. No accept on this grid depends on the step while the ceiling is 5 (the rule binds only where the walk is under 130 updates, and elsewhere the ceiling does), so the step is the coarsest with no cap excess, and it exists to bound the cost of a create: every update would be 8/(bn*t) walks of four exact tests. The table is the cap each step gives against the dense walk (every update), as the largest relative excess over the full sweep's grid, 1e-4 to 0.12 (a sampled walk can only miss a violation, so it is never below). ## 3. Review — findings, with verdicts

- **F1 · BY DESIGN** — **The steady-state gains are the optimal ones for the discrete model, not the fixed-gain flavor's.** At the bound `kr` is 9.2% larger and `kp` 5.3% smaller than the Butterworth gains of the same `bn*t`; the two meet as `bn*t` goes to 0 (§2.1). A caller who wants the Butterworth shape uses `StateFilter`.

- **F2 · BY DESIGN** — **The lower stability margin is thinner than the first flavor's.** At the bound the lower edge is 0.365, 2.7 dB under `K_MIN` (the first flavor: 4.1 dB). A detector slope that compresses to about 0.58 of nominal at low signal-to-noise ratio is inside it, closer to the edge than `StateFilter` would put it (§2.2, §2.4).

- **F3 · BY DESIGN** — **How much high-gain start is allowed depends on `bn*t`, and it is the ceiling that binds up to 0.04.** The rule's own cap is about 6 for every `bn*t` up to 0.04 and falls past it (3.8 at 0.06, 2.4 at 0.08, 1.2 at the bound); the constructor accepts at most 5, so the ceiling binds up to 0.04 and the rule past it (§2.5). A fast loop (large `bn*t`) gets the least acquisition from a start.

- **F4 · BY DESIGN** — **A start past the ceiling settles a frequency step faster still, and did not diverge in any case measured.** At `bn*t = 0.01` the best start measured (30) settles in 191 updates against 703 for the steady state (0.27); the validated constructors refuse it, and an embedder that passes a larger start through the unguarded init is outside the certified range (§2.6). Whether to relax the ceiling is the owner's.

- **F5 · GAP** — **The filter is certified as an object, not in a closed loop with a real discriminator and noise.** Pull-in and cycle slips under noise, a detector whose slope and output variance compress at low signal-to-noise ratio, the cost of a start above 1 at loops slower than `bn*t` 1e-4, the converging loop's stability against the frozen-gain prediction, a gallery figure and the embedders' adoption are not covered here. Tracked in [#3136](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3136).

- **F6 · C-ONLY** — **The plain covariance update against the wider reference, and the sampled start rule against the dense one,** are certified in C (`test_state_filter_kalman_core.c`, `state_filter_kalman_loop.c`): the binding does not expose `P` (§2.7).
