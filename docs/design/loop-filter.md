# The Loop Filter

Every tracking loop in doppler — carrier, code, symbol timing, gain — is the
same three pieces: a discriminator that says *how wrong*, a filter that turns
a noisy sequence of those into a stable estimate, and an oscillator or
interpolator that applies it. The discriminators are all different, because
what "wrong" means is different every time. The oscillators are the NCO and
the resampler. **The filter is one object, embedded by value in seven of
them.**

This page is the **why**: what the filter promises, what its one parameter
`bn` actually means, the condition under which that promise holds — which is
the part that is easy to violate and invisible when you do — and what it
deliberately does not do.

The contract lives in `native/inc/doppler/loop_filter/loop_filter_core.h` and the
C-level evidence in `native/tests/loop_filter/test_loop_filter_core.c`. This page does
not restate either; it explains the reasoning they assume.

Related: [The Exponential Moving Average](ema.md), [The NCO](nco.md),
[RateSync — timing](ratesync-timing.md), [MPSK Receiver](mpsk.md).

**Status.** The loop filter is **certified**: its validation report
(`src/doppler/track/tests/validation/loop_filter/results.md`, generated by the `validate.py` beside it) measures the
claims below through the binding, and `native/validation/loop_filter_noise_bw.c`
measures the closed loop's bandwidth. Sections 1–5 describe the shipped object
and the reasoning behind its present form. Section 6 states the boundaries the
header declares. **Section 8 is the list of questions this page set before the
characterization existed**, kept with what the measurement answered so the
questions stay visible beside their answers.

## 1. What it is for

One question, asked once per loop update: **given a noisy sequence of "how
wrong am I" measurements, what is my best running estimate of the error's
rate of change, and what correction should I apply right now?**

Two answers are wanted at once, and that is why the filter is second order
rather than a smoother:

- **the accumulated estimate** — a carrier offset, a symbol rate, a code
    rate. This is memory: it must persist when the error goes to zero,
    because zero error is exactly what a *correct* estimate produces. A loop
    whose correction decays to nothing when it stops seeing error cannot
    hold a frequency.
- **the instantaneous nudge** — the immediate response to this update's
    error, which is what gives the loop its speed and its damping.

The integrator is the first, `kp*e` is the second, and `control = integ + kp*e` is the sum. That is the whole object.

Seven objects embed it by value, through `dp_loop_filter_init()` or
`dp_loop_filter_configure()`:

| object             | what its integrator holds      | update period          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| `costas`           | carrier frequency offset       | one symbol             |
| `carrier_mpsk`     | carrier frequency offset       | one symbol             |
| `carrier_nda`      | carrier frequency offset       | one discriminator look |
| `dll`              | code rate                      | one code period        |
| `symsync`          | symbol timing rate             | one symbol             |
| `ratesync`         | symbol timing rate             | one symbol             |
| `burst_despreader` | carrier **and** code, one each | per burst update       |

`mpsk_receiver` embeds it twice more through `mpsk_rx_loops_t`, once for
carrier and once by way of `ratesync_loop_t`.

Seven consumers, one recursion — for the same reason the EMA is one
function. A property established for one private copy says nothing about
the others, and a defect fixed in one silently leaves the rest wrong; that
history is written up in [The NCO](nco.md), which had three copies of a
phase-word conversion with one of them fixed.

## 2. Theory of operation

The recurrence is two lines:

```text
integ   += ki * e
control  = integ + kp * e
```

The gains are not free parameters. They are derived from a **loop noise
bandwidth** `bn`, a damping factor `zeta` and an update period `t`, so that
a caller specifies the loop's behaviour in units it can reason about rather
than by tuning two numbers with no physical meaning.

### 2.1 Where the gains come from

For the continuous-time second-order loop, the noise bandwidth and the
natural frequency are related by the standard result

```text
Bn = wn * (zeta + 1 / (4*zeta)) / 2
```

`dp_loop_filter_init()` **inverts** it — that single line is the entire reason
the parameter is called a noise bandwidth:

```text
wn = 8 * zeta * bn / (4*zeta^2 + 1)
```

The discrete gains then follow the canonical bilinear-mapped form, with
`th = wn*t` and `den = 4 + 4*zeta*th + th^2`:

```text
kp = 8 * zeta * th / den
ki = 4 * th^2      / den
```

### 2.2 It is the textbook form, written differently

That last pair does not look like the form in the standard reference
(Rice, *Digital Communications: A Discrete-Time Approach*, App. C), which
is usually quoted as

```text
theta = 4 * zeta * Bn * T / (4*zeta^2 + 1)
kp    = 4 * zeta * theta        / (1 + 2*zeta*theta + theta^2)
ki    = 4 * theta^2             / (1 + 2*zeta*theta + theta^2)
```

The two **are the same expression**. doppler's `th` is exactly `2*theta`,
and substituting it through turns one into the other identically — not
approximately, and not only for small bandwidths. Confirmed to machine
precision across `bn` from 0.001 to 0.2.

This matters for a practical reason: it means the implementation can be
checked against an *independently written* expression rather than against a
transcription of itself. A test that re-types
`8*zeta*th/(4 + 4*zeta*th + th*th)` beside the implementation's own
`8*zeta*th/(4 + 4*zeta*th + th*th)` proves only that the file was copied
correctly. Asserting the Rice parameterisation instead is a real check,
because a sign or factor error in one form does not reproduce in the other.

## 3. The promise holds only in the loop's own units

`bn` is the noise bandwidth **of the closed loop**, and the derivation above
assumes the rest of the loop has unit gain: that the discriminator returns
one unit of error per unit of the quantity being tracked, and the oscillator
applies one unit of correction per unit of control. In the textbook that is
the condition `Kd * K0 = 1`.

Nothing in `loop_filter_core.h` can enforce this — the filter never sees the
discriminator or the oscillator. **It is the caller's obligation, and it is
the single most consequential thing about this object.** A discriminator
whose slope is 4 delivers a loop four times wider than the `bn` its
constructor was handed, and nothing anywhere reports the discrepancy: the
loop still tracks, just with the wrong bandwidth, the wrong settling time
and the wrong noise.

This is why doppler's discriminators normalise by their own contribution,
and it is worth seeing that the rule was arrived at independently three
times before it was recognised as one rule:

- `carrier_nda_disc()` divides out its own `|z|^M`, so its slope does not
    depend on the amplitude reaching it;
- `ratesync` scales by `dp_symsync_ted_slope()` — the TED's *only*
    normalisation is its own slope, which is [MPSK Receiver](mpsk.md) §6.1;
- the AGC exists in front of both so the amplitude a discriminator sees is
    one.

Read together, those are not three tuning decisions. They are three
instances of making `Kd * K0 = 1` true so that `bn` means what it says.

The corollary is the failure mode to look for first when a loop is wider or
narrower than it was configured to be: suspect the discriminator's slope
before suspecting these six lines of arithmetic.

## 4. The embedding contract

The state struct is **public**, and that is a design decision rather than an
oversight. A tracker holds one by value — often two — and a heap allocation
per loop inside an object that already owns its own allocation buys nothing
and costs an indirection in the hot path. `dp_loop_filter_create()` exists for
the Python binding, and is `calloc` plus `dp_loop_filter_init()`.

The contract that follows from embedding is small and sharp:

**`dp_loop_filter_init()` does not touch `integ`.** That is what makes it double
as a retune (§5) — but it also means an embedder is responsible for the
integrator's initial value, and a `dp_loop_filter_state_t` on the stack starts
with whatever was there.

Every embedder in the tree honours this, and — audited rather than assumed —
they do it in three different ways:

| how                                             | who                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `dp_loop_filter_reset()` after `init`           | `dll`, `ratesync`, `mpsk_receiver`, `burst_despreader` (code loop) |
| assign a **seeded** value outright              | `costas`, `carrier_mpsk`, `burst_despreader` (carrier loop)        |
| `memset` the whole enclosing struct before init | `symsync`                                                          |

The second row is the interesting one, and the reason `init` must not zero:
`costas` and `carrier_mpsk` seed the integrator to
`init_norm_freq * 2*pi * tsamps`, because the integrator *is* the frequency
estimate (§1) and a caller who already knows roughly where the carrier is
should not make the loop rediscover it. Zeroing on init would throw that
away.

`dll_core.c` is the one that documents the hazard in place, having evidently
met it — its `dp_loop_filter_reset()` carries the comment that an embedded state
"would otherwise start with a garbage code rate".

So the contract is real, currently honoured everywhere, and **unpinned by any
test** — which makes it exactly the kind of thing that holds until the eighth
embedder arrives. The object could remove the hazard by zeroing in `init` and
giving the retune its own entry point, but that would break both the seeding
pattern above and the retune-preserves-lock property `ratesync` depends on.
The split is deliberate; the burden is real; the answer is to pin it.

## 5. Two ways to change a running loop, and they are not the same

- **`dp_loop_filter_configure(bn, zeta, t)`** recomputes the gains and leaves
    the integrator alone. This is the acquisition-to-tracking transition: run
    wide to pull in, then narrow to reduce noise, **without losing the
    frequency estimate you just spent the acquisition earning**. `ratesync`
    retunes exactly this way.
- **`dp_loop_filter_reset()`** zeroes the integrator and leaves the gains alone.
    This is dropping lock: the estimate is now believed to be wrong, the
    bandwidth is still right, start again.

They are orthogonal on purpose, and every consumer uses both. Calling the
wrong one is a real bug with a quiet signature — a `reset` where a
`configure` was meant throws away a good frequency estimate and looks like a
loop that will not hold lock.

## 6. The boundaries the header declares

`dp_loop_filter_init()`'s documentation constrains two of its three parameters:
`bn >= 0` and `t > 0`. `zeta` is described as "typically 0.707" without a
stated range.

**They are one predicate, `dp_loop_filter_params_ok()`, which
`dp_loop_filter_create()` and `dp_loop_filter_configure()` call and
`dp_loop_filter_init()` deliberately does not**
([gh-740](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/740), doppler#2103).
That split is the design, so it is worth stating why rather than leaving it
to look like an omission:

- **`create()` is the untrusted boundary.** `LoopFilter(bn, zeta, t)` hands
    a Python caller's arbitrary doubles straight to it. Before the guard,
    `t = 0` produced `kp = ki = 0` — a dead loop indistinguishable from the
    legitimate frozen `bn = 0` below — and `t = inf`, or a NaN in any
    argument, produced NaN gains, which poison every subsequent update
    permanently. Both were one line away in Python. It now returns NULL for
    `bn < 0`, `zeta <= 0`, `t <= 0` or any non-finite argument, and the
    binding raises `ValueError` with the component's own message.
    `configure()` is the same boundary for a retune —
    `LoopFilter.configure(...)` hands the same arbitrary doubles to it — so it
    returns `DP_ERR_INVALID` on the same domain and leaves the loop as it was.
- **`init()` is the by-value path, and its callers check first.** An
    embedder that takes these numbers from a caller calls
    `dp_loop_filter_params_ok()` before `init()` (the header lists which);
    the only runtime-computed `t` in the tree is `mpsk_receiver`'s
    `1.0/upd`, safe because `m_out >= 2` is checked in its own constructor.
    Guarding `init()` again would be error handling for a case its callers
    have already excluded.

Validating in `create()` also makes the arithmetic **total**. With
`bn >= 0` and `zeta > 0` the intermediate `th` is non-negative, so
`den = 4 + 4*zeta*th + th^2 >= 4` — which closes the one genuinely
pathological corner: for `zeta >= 1` a sufficiently negative `bn` drives
`den` exactly through zero and both gains to infinity.

`bn = 0` remains **inside** the domain and is accepted: it means a frozen
loop, and that is how a loop is held open.

`t` is an update *period in samples*, not a rate, and `bn` is normalised to
**cycles per sample of the update clock** — so a loop updating once per
symbol at 8 samples/symbol and a loop updating every sample do not mean the
same thing by `bn = 0.01`. This is the units seam that
[MPSK Receiver](mpsk.md) §7 exists to pin down, and where `mpsk_receiver`
passes `1.0/upd` rather than `1.0`.

## 7. State

The filter is a pointer-free POD, so its serialized state is a whole-struct
snapshot (`DP_DEFINE_POD_STATE`) rather than a hand-packed field list. The
practical consequence is that **the blob carries the configuration as well as
the memory** — `bn`, `zeta`, `t`, `kp` and `ki` travel with `integ`.

For the documented use — restore into an identically-built instance — that is
exactly right and costs nothing. What it means when the instance is *not*
identically built is [section 8](#8-what-the-characterization-set-out-to-measure)'s
last question.

## 8. What the characterization set out to measure

Written before the characterization existed, so that the sweep would answer
these rather than confirm them. Each now has an answer in the validation
report:

1. **Is `bn` the loop's noise bandwidth?** **Yes, measured** (report §2.2–§2.4,
    findings `bn_loop_noise_bandwidth_claim`, `bandwidth_error_is_law` and
    `t_drops_out_of_error`). The delivered bandwidth is always slightly wide, by a closed-form
    `16·zeta²/(4·zeta²+1)² · (bn·t)`, and only the product `bn·t` matters.
1. ~~**What happens outside the declared domain.**~~ **Answered, and the
    answer became a fix.** `t = 0` produced a dead loop indistinguishable
    from the legitimate frozen `bn = 0`, and `t = inf` or any NaN produced
    NaN gains that poison the object permanently — both reachable from
    Python in one line. `create()` and `configure()` now reject them (§6,
    gh-740); `init()` is deliberately still unguarded.
1. **`dp_loop_filter_steps()` against `dp_loop_filter_step()`.** **Pinned.**
    `test_loop_filter_core.c` §8–§9 assert per-element equivalence, the
    integrator carrying across calls, and `out` aliasing `x`; the report's
    §2.10 repeats them through the binding.
1. **Does the retune actually preserve lock?** The report's §2.7 measures the
    arithmetic half — `configure` leaves the estimate bit-identical. Whether a
    mid-track retune is *transient-free* in the tracking sense is a
    closed-loop claim this object's report does not make.
1. **Restore into a differently-configured instance** — §7. **Measured**
    (report §2.9, finding `restore_carries_config`): the restore carries the source's configuration and
    retunes the target.

## 9. What it deliberately is not

- **It is not a smoother, and an EMA is not a substitute.** The distinction
    is load-bearing and conflating the two costs a transient; that is
    [The Exponential Moving Average](ema.md) §7, written as the diagnosis of
    a measured defect in the AGC's decimated loop.
- **There is no anti-windup.** The integrator accumulates without bound. A
    loop driven by a discriminator that is saturated or meaningless will wind
    up, and recovering takes as long as the winding did. Every consumer that
    needs bounded behaviour must bound its own discriminator or its own
    control.
- **There is no output limit,** and adding one is not free: a bound on the
    control can stop the oscillator, and a stopped oscillator in a
    strobe-driven loop never receives the update that would restart it. That
    is [The NCO](nco.md)'s cycle-slip argument, and it is why
    `nco_steer_scale` exists without a production caller.
- **It does not know its own update rate.** `t` is a number it was handed. A
    loop whose update clock changes — a handover from a fixed-rate
    acquisition tap to a symbol-rate decision-directed one — must re-`init`,
    and forgetting to is [MPSK Receiver](mpsk.md) §10's open item.

# Part II — the state filter

Sections 1–9 describe the second-order object. Part II describes its
third-order sibling, `StateFilter`: the use case that shaped it, its form,
the exact discrete-time derivation its gains come from, the domain it is
stable on, what embedding it costs, and what the sweep measured. The
contract is `native/inc/doppler/state_filter/state_filter_core.h` and the
evidence is
`src/doppler/track/tests/validation/state_filter/results.md`.

## 10. Use cases

1. **A narrow loop that must also follow a frequency ramp.** A carrier loop
    that updates once per code period (250 to 318 microseconds) on a signal
    whose carrier drifts at 400 Hz/s, run at a signal-to-noise ratio near
    4 dB per symbol. The loop needs a narrow noise bandwidth (`bn*t` about
    0.01) to ride out the noise and no steady phase error on a frequency
    ramp. The second-order filter cannot do both: its steady phase error
    under a ramp of `r` cycles per update per update is `r/ki` (§14), and
    `ki` falls as the square of the bandwidth. At `bn*t = 0.01` the
    lag is tens of degrees at the ramp rates a satellite-class Doppler
    produces, so the loop either loses the ramp or, widened to hold it,
    slips on noise. The caller that needs this is the asynchronous DSSS
    receiver's pre-despread carrier loop; adopting the filter there is its
    own work, after this object is certified.
1. **A rate-aided oscillator.** Any loop whose controlled quantity has a
    steady slope (a Doppler rate, a clock drift rate) and that would
    otherwise carry the lag of use case 1.
1. **Everything that is second order today stays second order.** The
    seven embedders of §1 are not asked to change, and §13 says why that
    is the constraint that shapes the struct.

## 11. The form

**Structure.** The filter adds one accumulator, in cascade with the
integrator. With `e` the error and `c` the control:

```text
rate  += kr * e              the rate estimate
integ += rate + ki * e       the frequency estimate (as today, plus rate)
c      = integ + kp * e
```

so `F(z) = kp + ki/(1-z^-1) + kr/(1-z^-1)^2`. `integ` keeps its meaning
(§1, the running frequency estimate); `rate` is its slope, in the caller's
control units per update. A caller who knows a Doppler rate may seed it
(§13).

**The loop it sits in** is the one the existing harness assumes (§2, and
`native/validation/loop_filter_noise_bw.c`): a unit detector, the filter,
and an oscillator that accumulates the control, plus a transport delay of
`d` updates in the error path (`d = 0` is the canonical loop and the only
one the second-order header's bandwidth figure describes). With
`u = z^-1` the characteristic polynomial is

```text
(1-u)^3 + u^(1+d) * [ kp*(1-u)^2 + ki*(1-u) + kr ] = 0
```

and `H = L/(1+L)` with `L = F(z) * u^(1+d) / (1-u)`. At `d = 0` the
substitution `w = z - 1` writes it as `w^3 + kp*w^2 + ki*z*w + kr*z^2`, so
the three gains are linear in the three coefficients of any desired
discrete pole polynomial: placing the roots is a linear solve, not a fit.

**The shape, and why the caller gives only `bn`.** Order 3 has no damping
argument: the shape of the loop is fixed by the design and the caller gives
`bn` and `t`. The shape is the analog prototype
`s^3 + b*w0*s^2 + a*w0^2*s + w0^3` with `a = b = 2`, the third-order
Butterworth polynomial `(s + w0)(s^2 + w0*s + w0^2)`: one real pole and a
pair at the same radius, 60 degrees off the negative real axis. The noise
bandwidth of the prototype is
`Bn = w0 * (a*b^2 + a^2 - b) / (4*(a*b - 1))`, which is `(5/6) * w0` for
this shape. The two constants live in one place in the header, so another
shape is a change of two numbers and a rerun of the validator. §12 compares
this shape with two others on the one property the automatic case depends on.

### 11.1 The exact discrete derivation

Written out so it can be checked, and held against the Parseval sum by the
C test and the validator (agreement to about `1e-9`). It does not use the
analog relation between `wn` and `Bn`.

1. **The loop.** The detector reads the error `w[n] - phi[n]` `d` updates
    late (`e[n] = w[n-d] - phi[n-d]`), the filter of §11, and the oscillator
    `phi[n+1] = phi[n] + c[n]`.

1. **The transfer function.** With `u = z^-1`, `L = F(z) u^(1+d) / (1-u)`
    and `H = L / (1 + L)`. At `d = 0`, with \`N(z) = kp (z-1)^2 + ki z (z-1)

    - kr z^2` for order 3 (`N = kp (z-1) + ki z`for order 2),`H = N / D`with`D = (z-1)^3 + N` (`D = (z-1)^2 + N\`).

1. **The noise bandwidth is Parseval on the impulse response.**
    `Bn = (1/2) * sum_n h[n]^2`, cycles per update, exactly (the harness
    computes this sum by running the real step). Write the loop as a linear
    system `x[n+1] = A x[n] + b w[n]`, `y = c x + d w` whose state is what
    the simulation carries: the oscillator phase, the frequency estimate,
    the rate estimate and the detector's delay line. Then
    `sum h^2 = d^2 + c P c^T` where `P` solves the discrete Lyapunov
    equation `P = A P A^T + b b^T`. That is a finite linear solve, so the
    answer is algebraic in the gains, not a series. Written this way a slow
    loop is a matrix close to the identity, every entry a small combination
    of the gains and of 1; the polynomial form `D(z)` above holds the same
    loop as large coefficients that nearly cancel, and loses the digits the
    small gains live in (a companion-form solve read 0.0008 for a loop of
    bandwidth 0.001).

    **Which phase.** The bandwidth is that of the oscillator phase at the
    detector instant, `phi[n]`. A phase read after the `kp*e` step of the same
    update is a different signal: the Costas lane, running this same loop,
    measured the sum of squares of that later phase 1% to 7% under `2*Bn`, so
    a figure compared with `Bn` must say where the phase is read.

1. **Stability is the decay of one sum.** The pair `(A, b)` is controllable,
    so `A` is stable exactly when `P = sum_k A^k b b^T (A^T)^k` converges, and
    that sum is the solution of the Lyapunov equation of the previous item.
    One computation gives both the bandwidth and the verdict.

    **Conditioning, and where the verdict is trusted.** The gains of a slow
    loop span many decades (`kr` is about `1e-9` where `kp` is `4e-3` at
    `bn*t = 1e-3`), and a Cholesky pivot of `P` in the raw coordinates can
    flip negative on a loop that is stable with a margin of `1e-4`. That is
    what happened to gains off the design manifold, the ones a converging
    Kalman filter holds (Part III): the verdict was erratic, a false "not
    stable" at isolated updates of a stable trajectory. Balancing the
    coordinates alone left 2 disagreements in 1404 judged cases, and a solve
    in extended precision alone left 5; and extended precision is not on every
    platform (`long double` is `double` on MSVC and on Apple's arm64). So the
    function no longer solves for `P` and tests it for positivity. It sums
    `P = sum_k A^k b b^T (A^T)^k` by doubling, `P <- P + A P A^T` with
    `A <- A A`, in balanced coordinates (a diagonal similarity by powers of
    two, which changes only the conditioning), all in `double`: every term of
    the sum is positive semidefinite (single entries may be negative), so the
    sum cannot lose definiteness by cancellation at any scale the gains span,
    and the loop is stable exactly when `A^(2^j)` goes to zero (the largest
    entry of the balanced power under 1e-12 is the stable verdict; non-finite
    or above 1e150 is not stable; 64 passes without either is not stable), which a pole at
    radius `1 - delta` does in about `log2 (40/delta)` passes (36 at `delta`
    1e-9). An unstable loop overflows or stalls at order one, and a marginal
    one (a gain at zero, a pole at `z = 1`) never goes to zero, which is the
    "not controllable reads as not stable" rule falling out of the same test.
    What the tests run: the exact bandwidth at delay 0 and loop gain 1 against
    the bandwidth asked for from `bn*t` 1e-6 to `STATE_FILTER_BNT_MAX` (1203
    cells, within 1e-9; not claimed below 1e-6, which is above
    `STATE_FILTER_BNT_MIN`); the verdict and the bandwidth against a
    simulation of the real step at `bn*t` 0.03 for delays 0, 0.5, 1, 1.25, 2
    and 3 and loop gains 1, 0.7 and 1.6; and a simulation of the loop's own
    state recursion over the Kalman recursion's gains at
    `bn*t` from 1e-3 to 0.08, starts of 1 to 5 times, at the four corners `K`
    in {0.5, 2} by delay in {0, 2}: 1404 cases judged, no disagreement, the
    same on every platform because there is one code path. The sweep over
    every `bn*t` from the floor to the bound, delays 0 to 4 and loop gains 0.5
    to 2 is not run. Two limits are stated, not hidden. A loop within about `1e-12` of the unit circle is
    within rounding of the edge, and either answer is within that rounding.
    A gain at or below zero reads as not stable by rule, which is the correct
    frozen verdict and is why a Kalman gain that crosses zero in a transient is
    reported as outside the rectangle there. The gains themselves
    (`create`, the blob check) come from the closed-form bisection and do not
    pass through this function, so a blob written on one platform is accepted
    on another to the last bit of the gains.

1. **Order 2, solved.** The Lyapunov equation gives
    `Bn = (2 kp^2 + (2 + kp) ki) / (2 kp (4 - 2 kp - ki))` and the loop is
    stable exactly when `kp > 0`, `ki > 0` and `2 kp + ki < 4` (Jury; the
    denominator is the last condition).

1. **Order 3, solved.** With `d1 = kp + ki + kr - 3`, `d2 = 3 - 2 kp - ki`,
    `d3 = kp - 1`, the loop is stable exactly when `kr > 0`,
    `4 kp + 2 ki + kr < 8`, `|d3| < 1` and `|d3^2 - 1| > |d3 d1 - d2|`, and

    ```text
    Bn = ( 2 ki^2 kp + 4 ki^2 + 4 ki kp^2 + 3 ki kp kr + 4 ki kr
           + 4 kp^2 kr + kp kr^2 - 4 kp kr + kr^2 )
         / ( 2 (ki kp + kp kr - kr) (8 - 2 ki - 4 kp - kr) )
    ```

1. **The gains.** The design fixes a shape, the two constants `a` and
    `b`; the gains are `kp = b*th`, `ki = a*th^2`, `kr = th^3`, the analog
    coefficients at `th = w0*t`. The scale `th` is then **solved** so that
    the exact `Bn` above equals `bn*t`: one monotone equation in one
    unknown, started from `th = 1.2 * bn*t` (the prototype's `w0 = bn / (5/6)`).
    The gains come from the closed-form order-3 bandwidth (`sf_bn3`, the
    delay-free expression of step 3) by bisection on `th`; the general exact
    function of §12, with its delay and gain arguments, is the independent
    check on that expression and is not what the gains are solved from. The delivered bandwidth is
    `bn` by construction, at `d = 0`, and the discretization only moves the
    shape. A matched-pole family (`z = exp(s*t)` on the analog roots) is the
    alternative way to fix the shape; §15 lists the choice.

1. **A delay.** An integer `d` replaces `u` by `u^(1+d)` in `L`: the
    companion form grows to `3 + d` states and step 3 is unchanged. A
    fractional `d = k + f` replaces `u^(1+d)` by `u^(1+k) * ((1-f) + f*u)`,
    linear interpolation of the detector's error between the two nearest
    updates; step 3 is unchanged on the larger polynomial, and the answer
    is exact for that model (§12), and for a real delay at integer `d`.

1. **The check.** Steps 1 to 4 applied to order 2 give the closed form
    above, the expression [#3121](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3121)'s
    exact second-order design is built on.

**What this means for order 2.** The shipped gains are the bilinear map of
the analog relation, and the closed form of step 4 evaluates their exact
delivered bandwidth, which becomes the stated figure in place of the fitted
closed form in the header: at zeta 0.707 the shipped loop is wide by 0.9% at
`bn*t = 0.01` and 3.6% at 0.04. The default does not change, and nothing in
the second-order struct, blob or any embedder moves with this work.
[#3121](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3121) asks for a
second, exact design for order 2 as an option for callers past the `bn*t`
budget; the exact bandwidth function of §12 is what it is built on, and its
interface is that issue's.

### 11.2 Cross-check against the legacy discrete-time design

#3121 carries a legacy design: a cubic in `kp` with `ki = kp^2 / (4 zeta^2)`,
reported not exact when measured (-0.018% at `bn` 0.01, -0.44% at 0.05,
-6.2% at 0.2). Its filter differs from this object's. Written as
`c[n] = c[n-1] + kp (e[n] - e[n-1]) + ki e[n-1]` it is
`F = kp + ki u / (1 - u)`, one update more delay on the integral path than
`F = kp + ki / (1 - u)`. The steps of §11.1 applied to each topology, with
`g = 1 / (4 zeta^2)` and `B = bn*t`:

| topology                | `H = N / D`, `D = (z-1)^2 + N` | exact `Bn`                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| this object             | `N = kp (z-1) + ki z`          | `(2 kp^2 + (2 + kp) ki) / (2 kp (4 - 2 kp - ki))`                  |
| legacy (issue's recipe) | `N = kp (z-1) + ki`            | `(ki^2 - 3 ki kp + 2 ki + 2 kp^2) / (2 (kp - ki) (4 - 2 kp + ki))` |

- **The issue's measurement is the exact `Bn` of the legacy topology.** The
    closed form reproduces its -0.0178%, -0.4403% and -6.2039% at `bn` 0.01,
    0.05 and 0.2 to the digits printed, so the residual is not a measurement
    artifact and not a model mismatch.
- **The legacy cubic is the exact solution of its own topology with one
    coefficient wrong.** Setting `Bn = B` with `ki = g kp^2` in the second
    row gives `(1 + 2B) kp^3 - 12 zeta^2 (1 + 2B) kp^2 + (4 zeta^2 (8B + 2) +   16 zeta^4 (4B + 2)) kp - 128 B zeta^4 = 0`. The legacy recipe has the
    same `kp` and constant coefficients and a different `kp^2` coefficient,
    `a = -4 zeta^2 (2B + 3) / (2B + 1)` where the exact one is
    `a = -12 zeta^2`. With that one coefficient replaced, the root measures
    zero error by Parseval to rounding (below `1e-11` percent at `bn` from
    0.001 to 0.2). The residual is therefore one coefficient of the formula,
    not a different loop.
- **This object's topology has an exact design under the same damping
    definition, and it is a quadratic**, not a cubic. Setting `Bn = B` with
    `ki = g kp^2` in the first row gives
    `g (1 + 2B) kp^2 + (2 + 2g + 4B) kp - 8B = 0`, solved by the positive
    root. Measured by the issue's reproducer (Parseval on `LoopFilter`'s
    recurrence), its error is zero to rounding at `bn` from 0.001 to 0.2
    (below `1e-10` percent). At zeta 0.707 and `bn` 0.05 it gives
    `kp = 0.12241`, against `0.12455` for the legacy cubic as issued,
    `0.12507` for its corrected root and `0.12473` for the shipped bilinear
    gains.

## 12. The domain, the stability bound, and the gain a loop may have

**One predicate.** The third-order create and configure refuse outside a
domain defined once, as `dp_loop_filter_params_ok` does for order 2
(`bn >= 0`, `t > 0`, finite, finite gains), plus a bound on `bn*t`. The
bound is not a tuning figure. It is derived from a stated requirement: the
loop at its nominal gain is stable, with its poles inside the unit circle,
and stays stable for every loop gain `K` (the discriminator slope times the
oscillator gain; §3) in a stated range, for a stated delay budget.

**A third-order loop is conditionally stable in `K`.** Scaling the loop
gain scales all three coefficients, and the Routh condition on the analog
prototype becomes `K > 1/(a*b)`: 0.25 of nominal is the lower edge
for every bandwidth, and nothing in the analog loop bounds `K` above. In
the sampled loop the delay bounds it above, roughly in proportion to
`1/(bn*t*(1+d))`, and a delay also nudges the lower edge up. A second-order
loop has no lower edge at all, so an order-2 embedder never had to ask.

**The range to design for.** `K_min` is the detector's slope at the lowest
per-update signal-to-noise ratio the caller specifies. The owner of the
caller sets that ratio; the slope at it comes from the detector's own
model, which is the embedder's, not this object's. A detector's slope falls below 1 as the
ratio falls, so `K` presents on the low side, which is the conditional side
of a third-order loop. One caller measures 0.58 at its floor and 0.72 to
0.76 in the mode it will use. The rectangle `K` from 0.5 to 2, `d` from 0 to
2 is the claim, with that reason: `K_MIN = 0.5` is the floor the automatic
case covers. The measured lower edge at the bound is 0.236 of nominal at a
delay of zero and 0.310 at two updates, 4.1 dB under that floor at the worst
delay and 5.4 dB under the 0.58 one caller measures.

The shape is judged first by its **downward** margin at `d_max`, and only
then by its transient. Arithmetic from the exact `H(z)` of §11.1 (gains solved
to the exact `bn*t`) gives the lower edge `K_low` of three shapes at `d` of
0, 1 and 2, and the price each pays in steady acceleration error (the gain
`kr / (bn*t)^3`, larger is better). The first row is a less damped shape
whose constants are chosen for the comparison; the other two are the
Butterworth polynomial of the design and the triple real pole
`(s + w0)^3`:

| shape (`a`, `b`)               | `Bn/w0` | `K_low` at `bn*t` 0.01 | at 0.04        | at 0.08        | `kr/(bn*t)^3` |
| ------------------------------ | ------- | ---------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| less damped (1.5, 2), chosen   | 0.7812  | 0.331, 0.334, 0.337    | .323 .335 .348 | .314 .337 .363 | 2.00          |
| Butterworth (2, 2), the design | 0.8333  | 0.249, 0.252, 0.255    | .244 .256 .268 | .240 .262 .288 | 1.65          |
| triple real pole (3, 3)        | 1.0312  | 0.111, 0.112, 0.113    | .110 .114 .118 | .109 .117 .126 | 0.86          |

Read against a detector that reaches 0.58: the less damped shape has about
4.6 dB of downward margin at best; the Butterworth shape has about 7 dB at
zero delay and 5.4 dB at the worst delay at the bound, and costs 18% of the
acceleration gain against the less damped shape (the steady error under a
given acceleration is 21% larger); the triple pole gives 14 dB and costs
more than half of it. That cost is what "stable in the automatic case" buys
in noise-bandwidth efficiency. The Butterworth shape is the design.

**At the bound itself** (`bn*t = 0.12`, Butterworth), the stable gain
interval `[K_low, K_high]` is `[0.236, 7.8]` at `d = 0`, `[0.268, 3.6]` at
1 and `[0.310, 2.1]` at 2 (and `[0.251, 7.3]` at 0.5, `[0.288, 2.9]` at 1.5
in the interpolation model). The margin that is left at the bound is
`0.58 / 0.310`, 5.4 dB downward, at the worst delay, and 2.1 upward: the
bound is set by the upward side, and the downward side has room.

**What the object can and cannot detect.** It can check, from its own
gains, that the nominal loop and the declared `K` range are stable at the
declared delay budget (a predicate on `bn*t`). It cannot see `K` or the
caller's delay: a detector of slope 0.3 delivers an unstable loop with no
error anywhere. The page's section 3 promise (unit gain) is therefore
stronger for order 3: for order 2 a wrong `K` changes the bandwidth, for
order 3 below an edge it breaks the loop.

**One function, one home.** The exact bandwidth and stability function
takes the gains and the order of the loop it describes (2 or 3), so it serves
order 2 as well. It lives with the state filter: a new component, so the
certified loop filter's code does not change in this work, and the loop
filter's header points to it. A second home would be a second copy of the
Lyapunov solve.

**The delay is not told to the object.** Two options: tell it (a `delay`
argument, gains and predicate accounting for it), or have the page tell the
embedder to budget it. The object is *not* told. A told delay
makes `bn*t` mean something different per caller, and the caller's delay is
rarely an integer (a mean-over-window detector contributes a fraction of an
update, and its window also narrows the loop). Instead the object **owns the
exact expression**: a public function that, for a given `(bn, t, order)`, a
delay in updates (exact at an integer, and the interpolation model of
§11.1 between integers) and a gain `K`, returns
the delivered noise bandwidth and whether the loop is stable. It is the
tool a caller sizes from, and the validator's sweep over `d` and `K` runs
through the same function, so the figures the page quotes come from the
shipped code. This applies to order 2 as well: the header's delay-free
figure is `d = 0`; a caller with one update of delay is wider than the
header says, which is the leading hypothesis for a measured shortfall in
loop SNR at the DSSS caller, and the page owes both orders as a function of
`d` in 0, 1, 2.

## 13. What is preserved, and the embedding cost

**The sibling form changes no byte of the second-order state, step or
benchmark, and the second-order gains stay as they are.**
[#3121](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3121) asks for an exact
second-order design as an option; that is a change to `LoopFilter` of its
own, and a re-derivation of the shipped gains would move every embedder's
numbers and every blob.

**The cost of changing the shared struct.** The second-order state is
public and embedded by value (§4). Adding `kr`, `rate` and an order to it
changes its size, and every embedder that snapshots it whole moves:

| embedder              | how it packs the filter             | blob that moves                            |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| the filter itself     | whole struct                        | `LPFL` v1                                  |
| `costas`              | whole-struct snapshot               | `COSTAS` v3                                |
| `carrier_mpsk`        | whole-struct snapshot               | `CARRIER_MPSK` v1                          |
| `carrier_nda`         | whole-struct snapshot               | `CARRIER_NDA` v5                           |
| `symsync`             | whole-struct snapshot               | `SYMSYNC` v5                               |
| `dll`                 | whole snapshot, plus `held_lf`      | `DLL` v15                                  |
| `burst_despreader`    | whole snapshot, two filters         | `BURST_DESPREADER` v3                      |
| `ratesync`            | field-wise, nests the filter's blob | loop v2, state v2 (via `LPFL`)             |
| `mpsk_receiver` loops | field-wise, nests the filter's blob | loops v7, receiver v7 and its real variant |

and every composition that nests those (the despreaders and the receivers
that compose them, through `despreader`, `dsss_receiver`,
`async_dsss_receiver`) changes size with them. The honest answer to "does
every composed blob in the library move" is yes, if the struct grows, and
the step would also gain a branch on the order in the hot path of every
tracking loop (`JM_FORCEINLINE JM_HOT`), which a benchmark row has to
prove costs nothing and could not be guaranteed to.

**The alternative: a sibling object, `StateFilter`.** The
second-order struct, its step, its blob magic (`LPFL`) and every embedder
stay as they are. The third-order filter is its own public struct (`kp`,
`ki`, `kr`, `integ`, `rate`, `bn`, `t`), its own step (the three lines of
§11, `JM_FORCEINLINE JM_HOT`, no order test), its own state triplet with
its own magic, its own create, configure, reset and predicate. An embedder
adopts it by holding that struct in place of, or beside, the second-order
one, and its blob moves **then and only then**: each adoption is a
version bump of one object, in the work that adopts it, with the
benchmark's order-2 rows unmoved by construction. This also keeps the
"certify the object, then the composition" order.

**Order is a choice of type, not a mode.** There is no create-time order
argument, no reject key, and no configure across orders, because there is
nothing for an order to switch. Within order 3, `configure(bn, t)` retunes
the gains and leaves `integ` and `rate`, as order 2 does (§5); `reset`
zeroes both accumulators and leaves the gains; the blob carries gains,
`bn`, `t`, `integ` and `rate`, and set-state accepts only a filter a live
one could be (the same rule as `dp_loop_filter_state_ok`). `integ` and
`rate` are public fields: a caller seeds `integ` (a known frequency) and
`rate` (a known rate) exactly as the second-order embedders seed `integ`
today, and a writable Python property for either is a mutator the #2022
gate probes, so both are in the blob. The header states the unit of `rate`
as it states `integ`'s: `integ` is the control per update, `rate` is its
change per update, so a Doppler rate of `R` Hz/s at an update period `T`
seconds is `rate = R * T^2` in cycles per update per update, when the
control is in cycles per update.

**An embedder that holds both orders.** Acquiring on order 2 and tracking
on order 3 is the obvious use, and at the switch the embedder carries
`integ` (the frequency estimate the acquisition earned, as `configure` does
in §5), zeroes `rate`, and seeds it only if it has an independent estimate.
Zeroing is the default because a rate learned by a wide second-order loop
is not available to carry. Whether pull-in (§15, unknown 3) needs a seeded
rate is for the measurement to say.

**The name.** The sibling is `StateFilter`: a
filter over an explicit state vector (the frequency estimate and its rate),
not "LoopFilter3". The house pattern is the loop filter's own (component
`loop_filter`, header `loop_filter/loop_filter_core.h`, C names
`dp_loop_filter_*`, class `LoopFilter` in the `track` module), so the
component is `state_filter`, with C names under the `dp_state_filter_`
prefix and the class `StateFilter` in the same module. Nothing in the tree
is named `StateFilter`, `state_filter` or `kalman`, in any case or spelling,
so there is nothing to collide with or reuse. The second-order blob stays
byte-identical, and the question of an `order=` argument on `LoopFilter` is
closed: there is none.

**Reuse check.** Nothing in the tree is a second integrator. The
`bn_fll` assist in `costas` and `carrier_mpsk` is a first-order
frequency-error term fed into the same integrator; the `dll` aid adds an
external rate to a nominal code rate; the burst capture's rate
hypotheses are a search, not a tracker. The third-order filter is new.

### 13.1 One recurrence, two flavors

A Kalman flavor of the same object is planned and is not built. The
fixed-gain object is shaped so that adding it is a view and some fields,
not a reshaping.

**The recurrence.** The filter estimates the state `x = (integ, rate)` of
the quantity being tracked, with the oscillator's accumulated phase
correction as the third state of the loop. Each update predicts, then
corrects with the error `e` through a gain vector `g = (kp, ki, kr)`:

```text
predict   rate' = rate ;  integ' = integ + rate
correct   rate  = rate' + kr * e ;  integ = integ' + ki * e + kr * e
control   c     = integ + kp * e
```

(the three lines of §11 are this recurrence with the prediction folded in).
**The fixed-gain flavor holds `g` constant, solved from `bn` (§11.1).** A
Kalman flavor computes `g` each update from a covariance recursion given a
process-noise and a measurement-noise level. For a constant-acceleration
model the recursion converges to a constant `g`, the steady-state Kalman
gain of an alpha-beta-gamma tracker, which depends only on the ratio of the
two noise levels. So the fixed-gain loop is the steady-state case of the
Kalman recurrence at some noise ratio, and that ratio is what `bn` selects.
That correspondence is proved in the continuous limit and measured in
discrete time in §19: the two flavors meet exactly as `bn*t` goes to zero
and differ by a stated amount at the bounds.

**What the two share:** the state `(integ, rate)`, the step's signature
(`e` in, `c` out), the blob envelope and its `reset`, the caller's units
(§13), the seeding of `integ` and `rate`, and the exact bandwidth and
stability function of §12 evaluated at the CURRENT gains, which is what
lets a caller ask what a Kalman filter's gains deliver at any moment.
**What the Kalman flavor adds:** the covariance matrix as state, its
constructor's arguments (the two noise levels and an initial covariance),
and the per-update gain refresh. Nothing for it is added now: no
covariance field, no argument, no code.

**The hot step does not pay for the flavor.** The fixed-gain step stays the
three lines of §11, inline, with no flavor test: an embedder compiles that
and nothing else. A Kalman flavor does not change that step. It refreshes
the gain fields from its covariance recursion in its own function, called
before the same inline step, so the step reads the gains it always reads. A
view shares methods verbatim, so the binding's `step` cannot be both; the
binding path may call an out-of-line step that tests the flavor, which costs
a branch on the Python path only (a call that already costs a Python
dispatch) and leaves every C embedder's inline step and its benchmark rows
untouched. Whether `jm` lets a view's method call a differently named C
function is a question for phase 2 to answer against the manifest, not for
this page to assume; if it does not, the Kalman flavor is a separate type
(a difference in method signature is a separate type, and a gain refresh
that takes the noise levels per update is one).

**The blob carries a flavor key from version 1.** What a state filter's
blob describes depends on its flavor (a covariance has its own payload),
which is the case the state-serialization page calls a reject key: set-state
into a filter of one flavor refuses a blob of another, never reinterprets
it. So version 1 carries a `flavor` word in its extra header, `0` for
fixed-gain, and set-state refuses any other value. A Kalman flavor later
adds `flavor = 1` with its own payload under the same version, because it
changes no byte of flavor 0; without the key the first Kalman blob would
need a version bump to be told apart. The key is written, not stored in the
struct: the type implies it until a second flavor exists.

## 14. Closed forms a caller sizes from

Owned by the object, as `dp_loop_filter_wn` is for order 2, so nobody
re-derives them. All are exact for the discrete loop at `d = 0`, from the
final-value theorem on `1/(1 + L)`: the steady phase error to an input of
polynomial order `m`.

| input                                     | order 2    | order 3  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------- |
| phase step, frequency step                | 0          | 0        |
| frequency ramp `r` (cycles/update/update) | `r / ki`   | 0        |
| frequency acceleration `a` (per update^3) | `a*n / ki` | `a / kr` |

Under an acceleration `a` the second-order phase error is a ramp, growing
by `a / ki` per update, so a caller sizes a dwell from the time it takes
that ramp to reach the error it can tolerate. `r / ki` is the exact
discrete form of `2*pi*R / wn^2`; the two differ in
the first order of `wn*t`, and the exact one takes the gains the object
holds. The function takes the state and the input's order, so a caller does
not choose between the analog and the discrete form. In the units of use
case 1: a ramp of `R` Hz/s at an update period of `T` s is
`r = R * T^2` cycles per update per update.

## 15. The unknowns, and what the sweep measured

Written before the sweep, so that it answered them rather than confirmed a
decision. The sweep is `native/validation/state_filter_loop.c`, and the
report `src/doppler/track/tests/validation/state_filter/results.md`
measures the same through the binding.

1. **Delivered noise bandwidth against requested, as a function of `bn*t`
    and `d`.** Zero excess at `d = 0`, to 1e-8 or better across `bn*t` 0.001
    to 0.12. A detector delay widens the loop: at `bn*t = 0.04` the
    delivered bandwidth is 1.12 times `bn*t` at one update and 1.26 at two,
    and 1.42 and 2.31 at the bound. No closed form in `bn*t*d` was found;
    `dp_state_filter_loop_bandwidth` is the expression.
1. **The transient** to a frequency step: at the same `bn` the third-order
    loop settles in about 3.3 times the updates of the second-order loop and
    peaks about 9% higher (report §2.5). The price of the rate state; the
    ramp it nulls is the use case.
1. **Pull-in range** with a real discriminator is a property of the
    composition and is not measured on the filter
    ([#3123](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3123)).
1. **The steady error under a frequency acceleration** is `a/kr` against
    the simulated loop to 1e-4 (C test §8).
1. **Behavior at the stability bound.** Past the lower gain edge the
    unstable mode grows by 1e-5 to 4e-3 per update at the delay-1 cells
    swept, a slow growth; past the upper edge it grows too fast to measure
    over the window.
1. **Behavior at the lower gain edge, under noise,** with a detector whose
    slope compresses with signal-to-noise ratio is the composition's
    ([#3123](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3123)).
1. **Delay as a range.** The edges are not monotone in the interpolation
    model of §12, so the rectangle is verified on a grid; a pure fractional
    delay is not modeled
    ([#3123](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3123)).
1. **A detector that averages over its window** is the embedder's model; the
    object certifies the canonical loop and gives the embedder the expression
    for the rest.
1. **The gain family.** The gains are the analog coefficients at the solved
    scale; matched poles are the other way to fix the shape and are not
    compared ([#3123](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3123)).

# Part III — the Kalman flavor

Part II is a fixed-gain third-order loop. This part describes its second
flavor, `KalmanStateFilter`: the same loop, with the gains computed each
update from a covariance recursion instead of held constant. What it measured
is in its validation report
(`src/doppler/track/tests/validation/state_filter_kalman/results.md`); what
that report does not cover is
[#3136](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3136).

## 17. Use cases

1. **Acquire fast, then track narrow.** A fixed-gain loop has one bandwidth:
    wide enough to pull in or narrow enough to ride out noise. The Kalman
    gain is high while the state is uncertain and falls as the filter
    learns, to the steady-state gain of the noise it is given. A loop that
    starts with a frequency error of a few bin widths and must settle to a
    narrow bandwidth gets both from one object.
1. **A loop sized from the signal, not from a bandwidth.** The fixed-gain
    flavor is sized from `bn`, a design number. A caller who knows the
    detector's noise and how fast the carrier's rate can change gives those
    two figures and gets the bandwidth that is optimal for them (§18).
1. **The fixed-gain case stays.** The fixed-gain flavor's step, struct and
    blob do not move (§21); a caller who wants a constant loop keeps it.

## 18. The model, and what the caller gives

**The model.** The tracked quantity has a phase, a frequency and a rate:
`s = (theta, f, a)`, advancing as `theta' = theta + f`, `f' = f + a` and
`a' = a + w`, where `w` is white with variance `q` per update (a rate that
random-walks: the dynamics figure). The detector measures the phase error
`e = theta - phi` with white noise of variance `r` per update (the
measurement figure), `phi` being the oscillator's phase. The loop filter of
§11 is the observer form of this model. With the predicted state
`x = (phi, f_bar, a_bar)` it is

```text
x' = F x + K e,      F = [[1,1,0],[0,1,1],[0,0,1]],
                     K = (kp + ki + kr,  ki + 2*kr,  kr)
```

(checked: `F - K*H` with `H = (1,0,0)` has the characteristic polynomial of
§11.1 for every `(kp, ki, kr)`). A Kalman filter is the same recurrence with
`K` chosen each update:

```text
S  = P[0][0] + r                     innovation variance
K  = F P[:,0] / S
P' = F P F^T - S K K^T + Q,           Q = diag(0, 0, q)
```

with `P` the covariance of the predicted state. The three gains the step
reads are `kp = K0 - K1 + K2`, `ki = K1 - 2*K2`, `kr = K2`. The step itself is
Part II's three lines; the Kalman flavor adds the covariance update that
sets the gains before them.

**What the caller gives, in the loop's units.** Two numbers, both about the
loop's own error signal, so nothing has to be invented as a matrix:

| given     | meaning                                                 | units                                |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `sigma_e` | rms of the detector error when locked                   | the error's units, per update        |
| `sigma_a` | rms change of the rate per update (the dynamics figure) | control units per update, per update |

so `r = sigma_e^2` and `q = sigma_a^2`. A caller who knows a C/N0 and a
maximum jerk turns them into these two the way it already turns them into a
detector variance. The steady-state bandwidth follows from their ratio
alone: in the continuous limit `Bn = (5/6) * (q/r)^(1/6)` per update (§19),
and exactly by the solve of §11.1 applied to the steady-state gains.

**The automatic case: `bn` and `t` alone, as the fixed-gain flavor takes
them.** The noise ratio is solved so the steady-state loop's exact bandwidth
is `bn*t`, and the covariance starts at its steady state. The filter is then
a constant-gain loop at the **Riccati** steady-state gains, which are not the
fixed-gain flavor's: they differ by the percentages of §19. Its stability is
therefore shown for those gains and not inherited from the fixed-gain
flavor's (§20 does it, and the flavor has its own constants). From the
first sample it behaves as the fixed-gain loop does to within about 1% in
settling time (measured below), the difference being the few percent of a
gain.

**A larger start, and its cap.** A caller who gives a frequency uncertainty
`sigma_f0` larger than the steady state's `sigma_f` (the square root of the
steady-state covariance's frequency entry) starts with larger covariance and
therefore larger gains for a while. The constructor refuses a start whose
gains, at ANY update of the covariance's trajectory (not only the first),
leave the exact stability rectangle of §12 (`K` 0.5 to 2, delays 0 and 2).
That rule caps the start, and the cap is the object's own property, measured
by the validator from the shipped constructor
(the report,
`src/doppler/track/tests/validation/state_filter_kalman/results.md`, §2.5), not typed here: it is about 6 times `sigma_f` for every `bn*t` up to
0.04 and then falls (3.8 at 0.06, 2.4 at 0.08, 1.2 at the bound 0.12). The
constructor also has a ceiling, `START_MAX` = 5, so **the ceiling is what
binds up to `bn*t` 0.04 and the rule binds past it**: a fast loop gets the
least acquisition from a start, which is the opposite of where a caller
might want it. The rule is checked by sampling the trajectory (each test 1% of the elapsed
updates after the last, and at least one update, so every update of the first
100 is tested), and the report's sampling table measures that choice against
the dense walk: the same cap to the bisection at every `bn*t` of the grid, 0.4%
higher at a 10% step and 5% higher at a 50% step. The exact stability test is
reliable off the design manifold (§11.1), so a sample does not step over an
excursion.

What it buys, for a noise-free frequency step and the same final `bn*t`, is
in the report's §2.6: at `bn*t` 0.01 a start of 5 settles in 0.56 of the
fixed-gain loop's updates, and a start of 4 in 0.59; at 0.04, 0.51 and 0.56.
**Inside the ceiling the Kalman flavor settles a frequency step about 1.8
times faster than the fixed-gain loop and peaks at about half the error: use
case 1 is served by a factor under two.** A caller whose start is several bin
widths off (a hundred times `sigma_f`) is refused; the fixed-gain answer to
that case is to start wide and `configure` narrower (§5), which keeps the
estimates.

**What relaxing the ceiling buys, measured and not shipped.** The owner
ruled the rule stays as designed for the first version and asked what
relaxing it buys. The report's §2.6 measures starts of 10, 30 and 100 times
`sigma_f` (built through the embedder's unguarded `init_qr`, which the
validated constructors do not accept, so nothing shipped accepts them and an
embedder that passes one is outside the certified range): at `bn*t` 0.01 a
start of 30 settles a frequency step in 0.27 of the fixed-gain loop's
updates, against 0.56 at the ceiling, and no closed loop diverged or failed
to settle across starts 1 to 100, slopes 0.6, 0.76 and 1 and delays 0 to 2 at
`bn*t` 0.01 and 0.04. Frozen at the gains in force the loop does leave the
rectangle for a few updates past the cap; the converging loop did not
diverge. That is a measurement, not a guarantee, and whether to relax the
ceiling is the owner's.

## 19. The claim of §13.1, proved and corrected

§13.1 said the fixed-gain loop is the steady state of the Kalman recurrence
at some noise ratio. **In the continuous limit that is exact; in discrete
time it is not, and the difference grows with `bn*t`.**

*Continuous limit.* The steady-state Kalman-Bucy filter of a triple
integrator driven by white noise of intensity `q`, measured in noise of
intensity `r`, has its poles where the symmetric root locus
`1 - (q/r) / s^6 = 0` puts them: `s^6 = q/r`, six roots on a circle of radius
`w0 = (q/r)^(1/6)`, of which the three in the left half plane are
`-w0` and `w0 * (-1/2 +- j*sqrt(3)/2)`. That is
`(s + w0)(s^2 + w0*s + w0^2)`, the Butterworth polynomial of §11, with
`a = b = 2`. So the shape of the fixed-gain loop is the optimal-filter shape,
and `bn` selects the noise ratio through `Bn = (5/6) * w0`.

*Discrete time.* The steady-state gains of the discrete recursion above are
the solution of its Riccati equation, and they differ from the fixed-gain
loop's (analog coefficients at the solved scale) at the first order in
`th = w0*t`. Arithmetic from the Riccati solution in scratch (a
number a C oracle replaces: the implementation's test produces them and this
table is regenerated from it), at the noise ratio whose exact bandwidth is
`bn*t`, as the relative difference of each gain from the fixed-gain flavor's:

| `bn*t` | `kp`  | `ki`  | `kr`  |
| ------ | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0.01   | -0.5% | -0.2% | +0.7% |
| 0.04   | -2.0% | -0.7% | +3.0% |
| 0.12   | -5.3% | -1.5% | +9.3% |

The same to the third digit for process noise `diag(0, 0, q)` and for the
exact sampling of a continuous white jerk. So the two flavors **meet at the
limit and are separate objects in the finite loop**: the fixed-gain flavor is
the Butterworth loop, the Kalman flavor's steady state is the optimal one for
the discrete model, and they differ by a few percent of a gain at the
bandwidths the loop is used at. Two tests follow, and neither is equality:
the Kalman steady state equals an independent solution of the Riccati
equation (the oracle), and its difference from the fixed-gain gains falls as
`bn*t` falls, at the first order (a ratio bounded by a stated constant over
a decade).

## 20. Stability and bandwidth where the gains move

The exact function of §12 is a statement about a loop with gains held
constant. Evaluated at the Kalman flavor's current gains it says what the
loop would be if they stopped changing: its delivered bandwidth and its
stability over a detector delay and a loop gain. That is the one thing it
says. It does not say the time-varying loop is stable, and the page does not
claim it, with two stated exceptions.

- **The constants of its own.** The first flavor's rectangle was derived
    for the Butterworth gains and does not transfer to the Riccati ones: at
    the bound `kr` is 9% larger and `kp` 5% smaller. The harness derives the
    flavor's own from the exact function at its steady-state gains (the
    report, §2.2). At `bn*t = 0.12` the upper gain edge at a delay of two
    updates is 2.18 (the first flavor: 2.08) and the lower edge is 0.274,
    0.314 and 0.365 at delays 0, 1 and 2 (the first flavor: 0.236, 0.268 and
    0.310). The largest `bn*t` stable at `K = 2`, `d = 2` is 0.1302 (the first
    flavor: 0.1248), and the lower edge at the bound sits 2.7 dB under
    `K_MIN = 0.5` at the worst delay (the first flavor: 4.1 dB), 3.3 dB under
    the 0.58 one caller measures. So the Kalman flavor has its own named
    constants, and the rectangle (`bn*t` 0.12, gains 0.5 to 2, delay 2) is
    inside both bounds; the lower margin is the smaller and is stated beside
    the constant.
- **A matched model at unit gain.** The Kalman filter's error dynamics are
    exponentially stable when the model is observable and controllable,
    which the triple integrator with noise on its rate is, so the loop is
    stable throughout convergence when the noise figures describe the
    signal and the detector slope is 1.
- **The gains along the start are bounded.** The constructor refuses a
    start whose gains, at any update of the covariance's trajectory, fall
    outside the exact stability rectangle; §18 states the cap.

What is not claimed: stability during convergence at a detector slope other
than 1 or with a detector delay, where the covariance is wrong about the
loop. The report measures noise-free closed loops through slopes 0.6, 0.76 and
1 and delays 0 to 2 (§2.6); that is a measurement, not a promise, and the
converging loop's stability against the frozen-gain prediction is open
([#3136](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3136)).

## 21. How a flavor is built

**Its own component, calling the base.** The Kalman flavor is
`state_filter_kalman`, a component of its own with its own class,
`KalmanStateFilter`. It is not a view of `state_filter`: jm builds a view's
`step`, `steps` and state blob from its parent's and has no way to override
them (an override is refused as a duplicate member,
[just-makeit gh-1092](https://github.com/just-buildit/just-makeit/issues/1092),
on the pinned jm 0.126.1 and on 0.130.1), and the Kalman step and blob are
exactly what differ. Nothing is
copied across: the Kalman struct holds the base `dp_state_filter_state_t`
by value as its first member, and the loop's three lines, the exact
stability and the exact bandwidth are the base's own functions
(`dp_state_filter_step`, `dp_state_filter_loop_stable`,
`dp_state_filter_loop_bandwidth`). The read surface the two classes share
(`kp`, `ki`, `kr`, `integ`, `rate`, `bn`, `t`, `bandwidth`, `stable`) is
shared by forwarding: each is a thin C function or a field read through the
base member, so the two classes cannot drift.

**The state, typed, public, and embeddable by value.** The Kalman filter is
its own C type, whose first member is the base, followed by the six distinct
entries of the covariance `P`, its start `P0`, and the two figures `q` and
`r`. It is public, as the base is, because the same embedders (the DSSS
carrier loop among them) hold their filter by value: it has an `init` that
needs no heap, and `create`/`destroy` for the heap path. Its functions
(`init`, `create`, `destroy`, `configure`, `reset`, `step`, `steps` and the
state triplet) take the Kalman pointer, so the compiler refuses a C caller
who hands a Kalman filter to the base's `configure` or `set_state`; the
base's functions that only read (`stable`, `bandwidth` and the fields) accept
`&k->base`. The step is out of line: its covariance update runs once per loop
update, at a rate far under the sample rate, so there is no hot path to
protect.

**What a caller states.** The caller's figures, as the owner ruled them: the
rms detector error when locked `sigma_e`, the rms change of the rate per
update `sigma_a`, and an optional frequency uncertainty `sigma_f0` (0 =
absent), all in the loop's own units; the automatic case is `bn` and `t`
alone. In C these are two typed constructors (`create` from the figures,
`create_bn` from a bandwidth) over the internal `(q, r, start)` form. In
Python they are one class with optional arguments: the figures, or `bn`
alone, and both or neither is refused with an error that names the two forms.
No carrier-to-noise-density or jerk argument and no detector model is in the
filter; the guide shows the conversion a caller with a C/N0 and a jerk figure
makes, as an example and not an API.

**The hot step.** The fixed-gain step is untouched, and the fixed-gain
struct, its inline step and every embedder's size do not move: the Kalman
type is a different type. The Kalman step is its own function: the loop's
three lines on the gains in force, then the covariance update and the gain
refresh. The fixed-gain rows cannot move, since every file under
`native/*/state_filter/` and its generated binding is byte-identical to the
fixed-gain component's; the Kalman rows are `kalman_step`, `kalman_configure`,
`create_start_1e2` and `create_start_1e4`.

**Telemetry.** Neither flavor has `set_telemetry`, and that is decided, not
missed. A leaf filter has no probes of its own to publish: the loop that
embeds it attaches telemetry and publishes the loop's outputs (the control
value, the phase error, the frequency estimate), and the filter's state is
readable (`kp`, `ki`, `kr`, `integ`, `rate`, and for this flavor `sigma_f` and
the covariance start through the blob). One thing differs for the Kalman
flavor, and a composing loop must act on it: the fixed-gain filter's `kp`,
`ki` and `kr` are constants, so an embedder can publish them once at attach,
but the Kalman flavor's gain schedule moves, so they are running values and
an embedder that wants them as probes publishes them per update. That is a
line in the embedder's adoption ([#3136](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3136)),
not code here.

**The blob.** The Kalman blob has its own magic and version, so a fixed-gain
blob and a Kalman blob refuse each other at the envelope. The fixed-gain
blob's flavor and pad words (eight bytes, reserved at version 1) are read as
zero there and stay as built; the Kalman blob does not use them. Its payload
is the base's seven numbers, then `P`, `P0`, `q` and `r`. `P` is not
checkable by the gains-from-bandwidth rule the fixed-gain blob uses, so a
Kalman set-state accepts a blob only if `P` and `P0` are finite and positive
definite (a Cholesky), the figures are inside the flavor's domain, the gains
in force are the ones `P` and `r` give, and the bandwidth is the figures'.

## 22. Reuse

A search of the tree for a covariance or Riccati step (`covariance`,
`Riccati`, `Kalman`, `innovation`, `Cholesky`, the Lyapunov solve) finds
only `state_filter`'s own exact-bandwidth solve. There is nothing to reuse.
The Lyapunov solve of §11.1 and the Riccati update are different equations;
the 3-by-3 update here is new and small.

## 23. Where each question is answered

Each question the design raised, and where its answer is. Every number is in
the report (`src/doppler/track/tests/validation/state_filter_kalman/results.md`);
none is typed here.

1. **Convergence time against the fixed-gain loop's pull-in.** A start of 4
    times `sigma_f` settles a frequency step in 0.58 of the fixed-gain loop's
    updates at `bn*t` 0.02 (§2.6); the table of settling by start, slope and
    delay is the report's record.
1. **Wrong noise figures.** The delivered bandwidth goes as the sixth root of
    the noise ratio to 1.1% over ratios 0.01 to 100 (§2.3).
1. **Conditioning of the covariance update for slow loops.** The plain update
    is kept: against a copy in `long double` it stays positive definite at
    every update and its gains stay within 3e-11 down to `bn*t` 1e-6 (§2.7);
    the entry that loses digits is the small `p12`, which the gains do not
    feel.
1. **`r` and the slope both move with the signal-to-noise ratio.** The
    effective loop gain is the Kalman gain times the detector slope, so a slope
    of 0.6 to 0.76 is the loop gain `K` of the stability map (§2.4); what the
    filter does when `r` is wrong in the joint way a compressed detector makes
    it is a closed loop with noise and a real discriminator, which is open
    ([#3136](https://github.com/doppler-dsp/doppler/issues/3136)).
1. **The slope and delay range.** The filter at its steady state is stable at
    slopes 0.6, 0.76 and 1 and delays 0 to 2 at `bn*t` 0.04 (§2.4); the
    converging loop is measured noise-free (§2.6) and its stability against
    the frozen-gain prediction is open (#3136).
1. **The step's cost.** About 9 ns per update; a retune about 0.29 ms; a
    create with a start above 1 1.2 ms at `bn*t` 1e-2 and 5.7 ms at 1e-4 (bench
    rows `kalman_step`, `kalman_configure`, `create_start_1e2`,
    `create_start_1e4`, single runs on one machine).
