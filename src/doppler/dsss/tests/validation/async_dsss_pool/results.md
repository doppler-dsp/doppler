## 3. Review — findings, with verdicts

- **F1 · FIXED** — The SECOND arm waited for receiver 1 to be idle before seeding it on B by hand, and called a draw that never got there 'receiver 0 never tracked A', a failed check. From about 44 dB-Hz the pool's own search takes B first, so receiver 1 was never idle: in every such draw receiver 0 held A and receiver 1 held B, both within 0.2 chip with code lock, and no release had fired. The pool was right and the meter misread it; the full sweep had been red on it since the arm was written (`run-pc-8df79b4cc`'s shards). The harness now scores those draws from the pool's own seed (20 of 200 scored here, section 2.2) and fails only when receiver 0 does not hold A, pinned in `--check` by a seed-8 draw at 50 dB-Hz that goes red with the fix removed.

- **F2 · CONFIRMED** — A sidelobe ghost the fine hand-over carries onto its parent's own carrier is left by the ghost rule, as the header declares, and leaves only by the loss release once both its flags are down: while it decodes the parent's data it is held. 66 of 200 forced ghosts were still held at 10 s, 13 of 40 at k = 0 (section 2.1). This is the declared near-far limit; the rule that closes it is #3263's.

- **F3 · BY DESIGN** — Admitting k = 0 would release 59 of 200 distinct emitters on a strong one's carrier (section 2.2's counterfactual), 8 of them decoding: the rest sit 24 to 31 dB under a 70 dB-Hz neighbour, past the near-far limit, where B decodes nothing anyway. Against that, F2's ghosts hold a slot. Leaving k = 0 alone is the owner's ruling (2026-11-23, async-dsss-receiver.md, section 8); these numbers are the trade's measured cost, and the rule that would take it is #3263's.

- **F4 · GAP** — After a ghost release the search may seed the same cell again: its sidelobe rule models one clean code period, and a data transition in the parent's epoch lifts the sidelobes above it. The header states this as a rate; no harness measures it yet (#3263).

- **F5 · GAP** — The ghost rule's constants (a 0.2 s window, EPS and STILL 10 Hz, a code-lock fraction of 0.2) are #2529's design values, and the harness's 40 Hz image tolerance is a 3-sigma coverage choice on them. None is derived from the carrier loop, which the card's principle 4 asks for. ALONE's ε (section 2.3) is the measured carrier resolution that a derivation would start from. The rule's redesign is #3263.

- **F6 · CONFIRMED** — The near-far range falls short of the specification's 20 dB. On a strong emitter's carrier, a distinct one decodes in 28 of 40 draws at 20 dB below it, 16 of 40 at 24 dB and 0 of 40 at 31 dB (section 2.2), nearly all of them held. The deep search, which the pool shares, takes a weaker emitter only to about 14 dB below (`near-far-alone-9de3b8120`, async-dsss-receiver.md, section 7.2). #3067 carries the search's side and #3263 the pool's.

- **F7 · GAP** — ASYNC_DSSS_POOL_PERIODS_PER_JOIN = 16 is one host's measurement: `make bench-ab A=967275d78`, the push row at K = 9 and 16 threads, 54 to 43.5 ns a sample at 2.2 GHz. Neither the header nor a benchmark derives it, and the wake-up cost it trades against is per host (#2778).

- **F8 · GAP** — The pool sizes each receiver's symbol row by its own rule, not by `dp_async_dsss_receiver_steps_max_out()`, and does not read `consumed`. A receiver call is one code period, far under the bound, so `feed_period` always consumes today (#3398).

- **F9 · C-ONLY** — The controller's rules (duplicates, the ghost rule's tests, the seed order, the release latency, reset and the blob's forgeries) need the C state, its rings and a receiver seeded by hand, which the binding does not reach. Each is certified in the C test section 1 names.
